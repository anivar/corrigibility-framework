Corrigibility Framework

Structural Accountability for Digital and Epistemic Public Infrastructure

Working Papers: These are preprints shared for community feedback. Not yet peer-reviewed.
Corrigibility is the structural property of a system in which affected participants can detect, contest, and override systemic error. A system is corrigible if and only if it satisfies five jointly necessary conditions that close the feedback loop between system behavior and participant correction.

Core Thesis

Corrigibility is a structural precondition for public legitimacy in digital infrastructure. It is not equivalent to transparency, accountability, or openness in isolation. A system may publish documentation, provide APIs, or maintain oversight boards and yet remain structurally incorrigible if participants lack binding corrective leverage.

The five tests form an irreducible set. Failure of any single condition converts the infrastructure into an open-loop system where error accumulation cannot be structurally bounded. Partial compliance is functionally equivalent to total failure.

The framework does not guarantee fairness, efficiency, or optimal design. It establishes the minimal feedback architecture required for sustained systemic correction at population scale.
